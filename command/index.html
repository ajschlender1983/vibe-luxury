The trauma lens — PTSD & the mitochondria

The "nervous system on guard" the film opens with is, at its extreme, trauma. A growing research line ties chronic stress and PTSD to mitochondrial dysfunction, which is exactly where VIBE's measured effect sits. It's a large, underserved, emotionally urgent story, told honestly.

The scale

~13M

U.S. adults with PTSD in a given year (about 5%); roughly 6% will have it in their lifetime, and rates run far higher among veterans and first responders. Source: U.S. National Center for PTSD.

The link

Body-first

Trauma keeps the body in hyperarousal. Researchers (Picard, Mellon and others) increasingly associate PTSD and chronic stress with altered mitochondrial bioenergetics. VIBE works at that body level: nervous-system regulation plus the mitochondrial effect Steven measured.

Why it matters to the story

  • It completes the arc. Numbness → recognition → regulation is the trauma-recovery arc, told without hype.
  • Underserved and urgent. Veterans, first responders, trauma survivors — a group money and apps haven't solved.
  • Partnership surface. Veteran programs, trauma-informed clinics, nonprofits: mission-aligned distribution.

The guardrail (non-negotiable)

  • Not a medical claim. VIBE is wellness, not a PTSD treatment. Tell the story; never promise a cure.
  • Validate before you claim. The PTSD–mitochondria link is emerging research; cite it as such, and run the study before any clinical language.
  • Counsel first. Trauma and medical-adjacent language get legal review before anything goes public.

This is a story and a market, not a clinical claim. Keep it wellness-framed and honest until the research and counsel are in place.
